If you've owned in West Hollywood for 20 or 30 years, you've watched the city change around you. Now you're thinking about selling, maybe to downsize, maybe because a parent's condo needs to be sold, and the question is practical: what are today's WeHo buyers actually paying for, and how do I price and prepare so my place is the one they choose?

The answer starts with an honest look at the market.

## The Market Rewards Homes That Are Priced Right

West Hollywood has been a selective market. According to Redfin data cited by local market reports, the median sale price was about $1.01 million for the three months ending May 2026, down about 5.6% from a year earlier, with homes taking roughly 86 days to sell. Realtor.com data for June 2026 showed homes selling on average about 3% below asking, with a median of 59 days on market.

For a seller, that means two things. First, buyers have time to compare, so condition and presentation matter. Second, an overpriced listing tends to sit, and a stale listing in WeHo usually ends up selling for less than a well-priced one would have. Pricing right from the first day is the single most valuable decision you'll make.

## Know Who's Buying in WeHo

West Hollywood's housing stock is heavily condominiums, with a smaller pool of single-family homes in neighborhoods like the Norma Triangle and the streets near the Sunset Strip. Local condo data for the 90046 and 90069 ZIP codes showed a median condo price around $903,000 for 2025, at roughly $1,000 per square foot.

Many WeHo buyers are professionals, often moving from renting, who are choosing walkability and a lock-and-leave lifestyle. They compare your unit against every other listing within a few blocks, so the features that set yours apart need to show up in the photos, the description, and the first showing.

## What Buyers Pay a Premium For

Based on Redfin's feature data for West Hollywood and what tends to stand out in listings, these are the features worth highlighting, or improving where you can:

- **Design character.** Mid-century modern details and high or cathedral ceilings are among the features associated with stronger sale-to-list prices. If your home has them, make them the headline.
- **Outdoor space.** A front patio, a usable balcony, or a private terrace. In a dense city, outdoor square footage is a real differentiator.
- **Storage.** Built-ins, walk-in closets, and dedicated storage units. Downsizing buyers notice when there's nowhere to put things.
- **Parking.** Deeded, secured, or side-by-side spaces. In WeHo, parking is a common deal-breaker, so be specific about what conveys.
- **Light and condition.** Fresh paint, updated lighting, and a clean, cleared-out space photograph better and show better than a dated, full one.

## Get Your Condo Paperwork Ready Before Buyers Ask

Most of WeHo's housing stock is decades old, and condo buyers' lenders and attorneys look closely at the building, not just the unit. Before listing, gather:

- **HOA documents:** budget, reserve study, recent board minutes, and any pending or planned special assessments. Low reserves or a looming assessment will come up in negotiation.
- **Balcony and exterior inspection status.** California's SB 326 requires periodic inspections of balconies and other exterior elevated elements in many condo buildings. Know where your building stands.
- **Seismic retrofit status.** West Hollywood has a mandatory soft-story retrofit program, and the city has estimated retrofit costs ranging from about $40,000 to $160,000 per building. If your building is on the list, buyers will ask whether the work is done and how it was paid for.
- **Tenant status, if applicable.** If the unit is rented, West Hollywood's rent stabilization and tenant protection rules may affect how and when you can deliver it vacant. Talk to an attorney before you list.

## One Advantage Worth Knowing

The City of Los Angeles's Measure ULA transfer tax applies to sales within LA city limits. West Hollywood is its own incorporated city, so ULA doesn't apply to WeHo sales. Confirm the current county and city transfer taxes with your escrow officer, but for higher-value sales this difference can be significant when you compare your net to a similar sale across the city line.

## The Takeaway

Selling in West Hollywood today is about precision. Price from recent nearby sales, not from the market of a few years ago. Highlight the features buyers pay for: character, outdoor space, storage, and parking. And have your HOA, inspection, and retrofit paperwork ready before the first buyer asks. Do that, and your home competes on its strengths instead of its unanswered questions.
