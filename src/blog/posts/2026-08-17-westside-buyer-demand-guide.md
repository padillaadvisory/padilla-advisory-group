If your home sits anywhere between Beverly Hills and the beach, one thing to understand before you list is that "the Westside" isn't a single market. Brentwood, Pacific Palisades, Santa Monica, Mar Vista, and Venice each have their own buyer pool, price band, and pace, and pricing your home off the wrong neighborhood's comps is one of the more expensive mistakes a seller can make out here.

## Brentwood: A Market That's Cooled, But Still Commands a Premium

Brentwood has seen real softening, with median sale prices down significantly year-over-year through the middle of 2026 compared to the prior year. That doesn't mean the neighborhood lost its appeal, it means buyers are more selective and less willing to overpay for a property that isn't priced and presented correctly. If you're selling in Brentwood right now, accurate pricing against the most recent (not last year's) comps matters more than it did two years ago.

## Pacific Palisades: A Slower, More Selective Market

Homes in Pacific Palisades were taking a median of around 76 days to sell as of mid-2026, up from about 48 days the year before. That's a meaningful shift, and it tells you buyers here are taking their time and being more deliberate. A property that's priced right and shows well still moves, but a home that leans on the neighborhood's reputation alone to justify an aggressive price is more likely to sit.

## Santa Monica and Venice: Demand Concentrated at the Top

Santa Monica's median sale price has been running around $1.7 million over recent months. Venice tells a more interesting story: overall average home values have drifted down slightly year-over-year, even as median sale prices have climbed. That gap usually means demand is concentrated on well-positioned, move-in-ready properties at the top of the market, while less differentiated listings get overlooked. If your Venice home needs work, buyers are pricing that in more sharply than they were a couple of years ago.

## Mar Vista: The Value Play for Westside Buyers

Mar Vista's median has been sitting around $2.1 million, generally the more accessible entry point among these five neighborhoods. Buyers here are often people who want Westside access, schools, proximity, walkability, without paying Brentwood or Palisades prices. If you're selling in Mar Vista, that buyer is looking for value relative to its neighbors, and your marketing should say so directly.

## What This Means If You're the One Selling

None of this is a reason to panic about timing. It's a reason to make sure whoever is pricing your home is working from comps in your specific pocket of the Westside, not a general "Westside is hot" assumption that may be a year out of date by the time your listing goes live. A property in Mar Vista and a property in Pacific Palisades are not the same market, even though they're a fifteen-minute drive apart, and the buyer who wants one usually isn't shopping for the other.

## For Sellers Managing an Estate or a Life Transition

If you're selling a Westside home as part of a probate process, a divorce, or a move into senior living, the stakes on getting the comps right are higher, not lower. An estate or shared asset being priced off outdated or mismatched comps can mean leaving real money on the table at exactly the moment when getting full value matters most. This is where a second, current set of eyes on your specific pocket of the Westside is worth the conversation before a number goes on the listing.

## The Real Takeaway

The Westside's reputation as a strong market is still generally true, but "strong" looks different in Brentwood than it does in Venice or Mar Vista right now. Know which market you're actually in before you set a price.
